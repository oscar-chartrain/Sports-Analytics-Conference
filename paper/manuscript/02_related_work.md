# 2. Related Work

**Point importance and clutch in tennis.** Morris [ref: Morris 1977] defined the importance of a point as the change in match-win probability between winning and losing it, and showed that importance varies enormously across score states. Klaassen and Magnus [ref: K&M 2001] tested whether points in tennis are independent and identically distributed, and found a small but real departure: servers win slightly fewer points at important moments, an effect that is averaged across players, not attributed to individuals. Their later work [ref: K&M 2003] and Barnett and Clarke [ref: Barnett & Clarke 2005] give the recursive win-probability formulas that leverage calculations rely on. Building on Morris, Tennis Abstract publishes the Balanced Leverage Ratio [ref: Sackmann BLR], and Stats Perform presented leverage- and momentum-based tennis metrics at this conference in 2022 [ref: Stats Perform 2022]. These metrics are now routinely cited in coverage of individual matches and players. To our knowledge, none of them reports how consistently it measures the same player twice.

**The clutch debate elsewhere.** Whether clutch performance is a stable individual trait is an old question in baseball. Cramer [ref: Cramer 1977] found little year-to-year consistency in hitters' clutch performance and argued that apparent clutch hitting is largely chance, a conclusion that later analyses have broadly supported with larger samples [ref: Tango, Lichtman & Dolphin 2007]. Basketball's long hot-hand literature [ref: Gilovich, Vallone & Tversky 1985; Bocskocsky, Ezekowitz & Stein 2014] shows the same pattern of debate: a widely held belief about individual performance under specific conditions, a statistical test that finds less than the belief implies, and a follow-up argument about whether the test could have detected it. Our paper makes the measurement question explicit for tennis: before asking whether clutch is real, we ask whether current metrics can measure it.

**Predictability and style.** Shannon entropy [ref: Shannon 1948] is a standard measure of how predictable a sequence of choices is, and has been used to describe variability in passing networks, play-calling and pitch selection [refs: e.g., game-theory pitch sequencing, SSAC 2023]. In tennis, previous SSAC work has modeled shot choice with deep learning [ref: Thinking the GOAT, SSAC 2023] and predicted shot outcomes from style and context [ref: Thin Edge of the Wedge, SSAC 2016]; neither links predictability to pressure. We use the conditional form of entropy, which measures how predictable a player's reply is given the shot they receive, and pair it with a leverage-weighted outcome. We found no prior work testing this pairing.

**Reliability.** Split-half reliability with the Spearman–Brown correction [refs: Spearman 1910; Brown 1910] and correction for attenuation [ref: Spearman 1904] are basic tools of psychometrics, where any instrument is expected to report how consistently it measures. Sports analytics has adopted the same idea as "stability" or "repeatability" for many statistics [ref: e.g., Tango et al. 2007], but, as far as we can find, not for leverage-based clutch metrics in tennis.
